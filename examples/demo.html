🧭 How it works

Training was slow because we recomputed the same image features every epoch. The trick: compute them once, keep them, and only redo the cheap scoring step.

Like a kitchen's mise en placeA chef doesn't chop onions again for every order. They prep everything once before service and then just grab it. Our backbone is the chopping; the cache is the prep tray. (Where it breaks: our "onions" never spoil, so the cache never expires unless an image changes.)
1. Load imagedisk → tensor
2. Feature cachecompute once, memory-map after
3. Compare to prompts8 phrasings of "damaged"
4. Scoremax patch similarity

Follow one image through it

  1. Input: screw_017.png, 1024×1024, with a thin scratch near the head.
  2. Features: the backbone turns it into [1369, 768] patch vectors (≈180 ms). In epoch 1 they're written to the cache; from epoch 2 on they're read back in ≈4 ms.
  3. Compare: each patch is matched against prompts like "a photo of a scratched screw". Scratch patches score 0.83; clean metal stays around 0.21.
  4. Result: image score 0.83 > threshold 0.5 → flagged as defective ✅

Why this design

Memory-mapped fileSurvives restarts, and the OS pages data in and out for us.✗ In-RAM dict: 14 GB, lost on crash
Key = file content hashAn edited image invalidates itself automatically.✗ Key = path: silently stale after edits
8-prompt ensembleAsking a panel instead of one judge smooths out wording quirks.✗ 16 prompts: no gain, slower
ℹ LimitsOnly helps when the backbone is frozen. If we fine-tune it, the cache must be rebuilt every epoch.
